The thesis develops and evaluates an obstacle-aware NMPC framework for the final berthing phase of a surface vessel operating at low speed in confined harbor environments. Final approach maneuvers are characterized by tight spatial margins, frequent constraint activation, reduced maneuverability, and sensitivity to modelling errors and uncertainty in the motion of surrounding traffic. To address these challenges, the proposed controller solves a finite-horizon nonlinear optimal control problem online at each sampling instant, using a 3-DOF-controllable vessel model (surge–sway–yaw) and explicit constraints for actuator limits and collision avoidance. Dynamic obstacles are handled via short-horizon online prediction based on simplified motion models (baseline constant-velocity with optional uncertainty inflation). Focus is given also to practical real-time operation: the work studies solver convergence and runtime variability, and assesses how warm-starting from a geometric planner (e.g., RRT*) increases docking performance. Safety enforcement is investigated through alternative formulations of hard distance constraints, soft constraints with slack variables, and control-barrier-function (CBF) based constraints. The method is evaluated in reproducible stress scenarios, all simulated in a MATLAB transposition of the map of the Helsinki harbor.
